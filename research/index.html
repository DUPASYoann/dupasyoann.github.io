MEFA

Fuse first. Detect next.

My role
First author

InputsRGB + IR + LiDAR
→
FusionAttention
→
DetectionYOLO / RT-DETR

The problem

In rain or fog, a single sensor may not provide a sufficient representation of the scene.

The approach

An attention-based early-fusion module combines RGB, infrared and LiDAR before an object detector. The study compares this approach with channel concatenation.

What the study shows

The work studies pedestrian and vehicle detection with YOLOv8 and RT-DETR in adverse weather. Fusion is placed upstream of single-modality detectors.

Source publication : MEFA: Multimodal Image Early Fusion with Attention Module for Pedestrian and Vehicle Detection

MEFA-MS

Make fusion more efficient.

My role
First author

InputsMultimodal
→
FusionU-Net + Attention
→
DetectionRT-DETR

The problem

MEFA’s inference time can limit real-world use. The trade-off between detection quality and model cost needs attention.

The approach

MEFA-MS introduces a U-Net encoder-decoder with spatial and channel attention, compared with MEFA on DENSE.

What the study shows

With RT-DETR on DENSE, the authors report 50% lower inference time than MEFA alongside improved mAP50. These findings apply to the paper’s experimental setup.

FMCAF

Explore how fusion generalizes.

My role
Coauthor

InputsRGB + IR
→
FusionFreq-Filter + Cross Attention
→
DetectionDetection

The problem

A fusion strategy suited to one setting does not necessarily transfer to different scenes or datasets.

The approach

FMCAF combines frequency filtering with cross-attention fusion to reduce redundant information and improve RGB–infrared feature sharing.

What the study shows

The architecture is evaluated on LLVIP for low-light pedestrians and VEDAI for aerial vehicles. These evaluations explore robustness in two different settings, without establishing universal generalization.

Source publication : Towards a Generalizable Fusion Architecture for Multimodal Object Detection
